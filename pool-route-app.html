When a pool route lives in your head

Tuesday is north side, Thursday is the spa circuit, Monday belongs to the two customers who always move their gate lock. Paper sheets get wet, group chats lose photos, and the moment you take a week off nobody can tell where stop #14 is or what chemicals go in it.

A pool route app should fix exactly that: the schedule, the stop list, and the history — in the pocket of whoever is holding the pole.

What Pool Route Book does with your week

Every pool gets a day, a time and a repeat rule, and your week assembles itself into a route grid. Tap a stop and you get the things you always re-ask: the address (opens in Apple Maps), gate and dog notes, equipment quirks, and every visit ever logged for that water.

One-off call about a green pool? Add it to today without disturbing the recurring route. Done for the day? The checkmarks you tick become the visit record.

Why not just use a generic route planner?

Field-service apps and route optimizers plan drives, not pools. They have no idea what free chlorine or CYA is, they need an office subscription and a login, and they assume a dispatcher sits between you and the work. Pool Route Book is one app, offline, for the person who owns the route and cleans the pools.

Questions pool techs ask

Is Pool Route Book free?

Yes — you can start free and run your first pools with no charge. Pool Route Pro (monthly, yearly or one-time Lifetime) unlocks unlimited pools, routes and reports. No account or credit card to try it.

Does the app work offline?

Yes. Routes, stops and visit logs are stored on your iPhone, so it works at the back of a yard with no signal. No cloud account required.

Is it on Android?

Pool Route Book is currently iPhone/iPad only.

Can I plan multiple days and recurring stops?

Yes. Each pool gets a weekday, time and repeat pattern; the week builds itself into a route grid, and one-off visits can be added to any day without changing the recurring schedule.
