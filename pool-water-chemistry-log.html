Numbers in a notebook protect nobody

The reading that matters is the one you can pull up six weeks later: what the chlorine held at after sunset, which pool keeps eating acid, when the spa last got a shock. Paper logs stay in the truck, and truck notebooks don't answer customer questions.

Readings, chemicals and history — per pool

Every visit log takes the four numbers that keep water honest (free chlorine, pH, total alkalinity, cyanuric acid) plus the chemicals you actually dosed and how much. It opens on your own default targets, so you're confirming, not keying. The pool's history page shows every reading since you started logging — trend problems announce themselves when the same pool keeps chasing pH.

The tracker that pays for itself in arguments

Green pool complaints, 'you must have shocked it to death', 'why does the water keep stinging' — every one of those conversations is short when you can open the log and read the last eight visits out loud. You were there, the water was in range, here's the record.

Questions pool techs ask

Do I need a subscription just to log water chemistry?

No — logging readings and chemicals is free to start. Pro unlocks unlimited pools and the full history.

Which readings can I track?

Free chlorine, pH, total alkalinity and cyanuric acid (CYA), plus the chemicals you added with amounts, and optional notes and photos per visit.

Can I see a trend for one pool?

Yes. Every pool keeps its complete visit history in the app, so repeated swings in pH or chlorine demand are visible at a glance.

Is this a pool math calculator?

It's not — Pool Route Book is the log and record layer: you test the water, the app keeps what you found and what you did about it.
